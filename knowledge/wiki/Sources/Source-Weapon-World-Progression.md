# Weapon progression across worlds

## Provenance

Owner reply captured on 2026-11-06, preserving the message as UTF-8 text with a
terminal newline. [[raw/2026-11-06/batch-06/weapon-world-progression.txt|Original reply]],
line 1; its SHA-256 is above. This adds detail to
[[wiki/Sources/Source-Equipment-Design-Intent]] without replacing the earlier direction.

## Summary

A new-world weapon should usually feel like a direct upgrade. An older weapon
with a unique ability and near-perfect rolls may compete with early drops from
the next world.

## Findings and evidence

- The general expectation is a direct upgrade from a new world's weapons most
  of the time.
- The described carryover exception combines a unique ability with near-perfect
  rolls; neither factor alone was specified as the exception.
- Early next-world drops are the comparison point. Competition is a possibility,
  not a guarantee that the old weapon matches every new-world option.
- No threshold for near-perfect, numerical power gap, or balance formula is given.

Evidence: [[raw/2026-11-06/batch-06/weapon-world-progression.txt|Owner statement]], line 1.

## Open questions

What roll-quality and performance comparisons define near-perfect and competitive?
The reply does not establish continued viability throughout the next world or
multiple later worlds. These are design targets, not verified current balance.
